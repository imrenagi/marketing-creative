# Voice, style and feel

Use this guide for style exploration or when a requested voice needs more than a wording adjustment. Styles are starting points, not a fixed menu; follow the user's own references and combinations.

## Define the differences that matter

Keep these dimensions distinct so variants do more than swap adjectives:

| Dimension | Useful choices |
| --- | --- |
| Speaker and perspective | Actual customer sharing an experience, creator recommending a useful find, founder explaining a decision, brand addressing the reader |
| Tone | Candid, warm, reflective, playful, reassuring, confident, matter-of-fact |
| Structure and rhythm | Small personal story, quick observation, conversational explanation, practical tip, objection and answer, concise offer |
| Intended feel | Recognized, curious, reassured, understood, excited, ready to act; an editorial aim, not a proven audience response |
| Selling intensity | Sharing with no ask, gentle recommendation, explicit invitation, direct purchase request |

Preserve shared facts, audience, approved concept, language and channel constraints. Vary the requested dimensions without changing the offer or inventing a different product experience. Do not generate every combination. If the user specifies only a count, select complementary approaches and briefly state your interpretation; do not require a full voice interview.

## Personal experience and useful finds

Write as the intended human speaker using their supplied notes, recording or draft. Draw out a specific situation, what they actually tried or noticed, why a detail mattered and their own reflection. Preserve the scale of the experience: one useful moment is not proof of a life transformation. Keep ordinary vocabulary and natural sentence variation; forced slang, fake typos, oversharing and repeated “honestly” do not establish authenticity.

A useful-find recommendation can focus on a small discovery and who might appreciate it. Distinguish discovering that a feature exists from personally using it. A founder can explain why they built something, but should not be rewritten as an independent customer. Retain supplied sponsorship or affiliate disclosures; do not disguise a commercial relationship as spontaneous endorsement.

Personal does not require first person. Direct, considerate language, a relatable situation and concrete product detail can feel personal without claiming an experience. When the brief contains only features, use that approach or request an anecdote if the user specifically needs a true first-person story. Proposed story beats or first-person placeholders belong in a labeled draft for speaker confirmation, not a purported finished testimonial.

## Distinct approaches

| Approach | What should change in the writing |
| --- | --- |
| Candid experience sharing | Begin inside a supplied moment; let the useful detail emerge through the story; end with reflection or a light recommendation when appropriate |
| Friend-to-friend useful find | Get to the discovery quickly, explain the practical reason it matters, and suggest who might find it useful; avoid a rehearsed testimonial arc |
| Warm practical explanation | Address the reader's situation, explain one mechanism clearly and give a useful takeaway without implying firsthand use |
| Playful or witty | Use a relevant contrast, image or rhythm; keep the product and benefit understandable, and avoid fabricated frustrations or results attributed to a real speaker |
| Direct selling | Lead with a supported benefit or relevant offer, answer the main objection with proof, make material terms clear and ask for the actual next action |

Selling intensity is independent of tone: a warm message can make a direct ask, and a candid story can end with an explicit purchase invitation. Use urgency only when there is a real supplied deadline or constraint. Do not add discounts, trials or guarantees to distinguish the sales version.

## Example: same facts, different feels

Fictional working material: Pinboard saves links with a short note and can search those notes. The speaker says, “I saved a train itinerary with the note ‘Friday train.’ Later I couldn't remember the website, but searching my note brought it back.” No price, time-saving measurement or offer is supplied. These examples illustrate voice, not performance.

- **Candid experience:** “I couldn't remember which website had my train itinerary. Turns out I'd saved it in Pinboard with the note ‘Friday train.’ Searched that, found it. The little note was the useful part.”
- **Useful find:** “A small thing I liked about Pinboard: I could search the note I'd added to a link. ‘Friday train’ got me back to my itinerary when I couldn't remember the website. Handy if that's how you remember things too.”
- **Warm practical:** “Sometimes you remember why you saved a link more easily than where it came from. Pinboard lets you add a short note and search it later, so the words that make sense to you can help you find it again.”
- **Direct selling:** “Find saved links by the notes you remember. Add a short note in Pinboard, then search it when you need the link again. Save your next link with Pinboard.”

The first two use the supplied anecdote. The others use known product behavior. None adds a price, broader result or fabricated personal history. In a real deliverable, match the last line to the supplied destination and requested CTA.

## Deliver a useful set

Give each version a stable ID and a compact label identifying its voice and feel. Put the complete copy underneath, with the channel's requested fields. Keep rationale and speaker-confirmation notes outside those fields. If asked for several complete posts or ads, do not return only hooks or a list of style suggestions.

Recommend based on the brief's purpose, not on a universal preference for sales copy. Read versions side by side: openings, pacing, narrative distance and endings should reflect the stated differences. If only the style changes, retain the argument; if several dimensions change together, call it exploration rather than an isolated A/B test. Revisions can request “keep version B's story, make the ending more direct” without restarting intake or replacing other versions.
