# Marketing brief contract

Scale detail to the assignment. Each section should contain decisions and evidence, not generic marketing advice.

| Field | Record |
| --- | --- |
| Objective | Business goal, desired behavior, primary metric, time horizon if supplied |
| Audience | Segment, situation, awareness, market, language, relevant research |
| Barrier | What prevents action; distinguish evidence from a hypothesis |
| Positioning | Category, alternatives, differentiated mechanism, supported outcomes |
| Proof | Exact claim, source, allowed wording, evidence asset, verification status |
| Offer | Actual price/eligibility/terms if relevant; unknown terms stay unknown |
| Action | One primary CTA, destination, what happens after the click |
| Distribution | Channel and specific placements, sound assumptions, ratios and duration variants |
| Resources | Brand assets, captures, people, budget/time constraints if supplied |
| Open decisions | Assumptions, material unknowns, owner or next evidence needed |

Keep creative latitude: “demonstrate the first useful task” is an execution requirement; “open with a blue phone spinning into frame” is a creative concept.

A good brief can support a demonstration, an objection-led story or an object metaphor without changing its factual foundation. Sources can be supplied product documentation, observed product behavior, research or campaign data. Record dates for changeable facts.

For a simple revision, provide the changed fields and affected downstream decisions; do not reproduce the entire campaign interview.
