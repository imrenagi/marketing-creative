# Creative concept contract

For each candidate, provide:

1. Stable ID, working title, central idea and audience insight.
2. Persuasive mechanism and relation to the marketing objective.
3. Opening: exact provisional hook and first visible action.
4. Narrative progression: what is revealed, demonstrated, believed and acted on.
5. Product attribution and evidence; mark assets or facts that are missing.
6. Ending: CTA and destination consistent with the offer.
7. Execution: likely format, visual approach, people/capture/generated asset needs.
8. Adaptation: how the idea works in every requested ratio and duration.
9. Test hypothesis: what result would support or weaken the idea; avoid fabricated scores.
10. Tradeoffs and production feasibility.

The selected creative brief preserves the concept ID, argument, proof, tone, essential moments, mandatory copy and adaptation decisions. Exact seconds, complete dialogue and camera coordinates belong to scripting/storyboarding.

Example distinction for a fictional task app: “watch a messy process become one completed task” demonstrates a mechanism; “answer the fear of migrating existing work” addresses an objection; “follow a handoff object through a team” tells a story. They can share the same objective without sharing the same opening or structure.

For 6-second and 30-second versions, state whether they retain the same argument or perform different jobs. A cutdown can need new copy; it is not automatically a sped-up long version.
