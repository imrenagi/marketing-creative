# Choose the argument by goal and audience

Complete: "After watching, [specific viewer] should believe [one change] strongly enough to [one action]." Choose the minimum evidence that could cause that change. Campaign objective, awareness, format, and duration are separate decisions.

| Goal | Viewer uncertainty | Argument and proof | Next step | Primary outcome; diagnostics |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Awareness | Why remember this? | Recognizable situation, distinctive brand cue, one memorable benefit; product tied to payoff | Remember or discover | Brand/ad-recall lift when measurable, incremental qualified reach; attention and attribution |
| Consideration | How does it help someone like me? | Situation → product action → useful output; differentiating mechanism | Explore a relevant demo | Qualified product engagement or consideration lift; demo consumption |
| Purchase / subscription | Why this, and why act? | Specific benefit, credible proof, major objection resolved, real offer and conditions | Buy, subscribe, or start a real trial | Cost per purchase, contribution economics; click/checkout funnel |
| App acquisition | Will it be useful after download? | Real workflow, first useful moment, understandable setup effort | Install and perform the first useful action | Cost per activated user, retention and downstream revenue; CPI is diagnostic |
| Leads / B2B | Is it relevant and worth my time? | Recognizable job, mechanism/demo, relevant evidence; qualify fit | Book a demo or request a specific resource | Qualified lead cost, attended meetings, pipeline; raw CPL can mislead |
| Retargeting | What still stops me? | Address one unresolved objection with new or stronger proof | Complete the relevant next step | Incremental conversions when measurable; CPA, conversion rate, frequency |
| Re-engagement | Why return? | Concrete use case, unresolved task, or verified improvement | Resume that task | Meaningful returning users; repeat use and retention |

Pick a measurable proxy if the ideal outcome is unavailable, and state what it cannot establish. One ad can contribute across the funnel but still needs a primary job.

## Awareness changes ordering

- Not considering the category: start with a familiar situation, useful lesson, or surprising demo. Establish relevance before demanding an install; integrate a recognizable brand cue.
- Problem-aware but skeptical: name the old approach's specific gap, then show a different mechanism. Avoid unsupported claims that all competitors fail.
- Solution-aware: show the task, clarify fit, and distinguish the alternative.
- Product-aware: lead with the actual objection, fresh proof, or real offer. Skip a long category introduction.
- Ready to act: clarify exact terms and next step. Urgency needs a true, relevant reason.

The deck's 30/30/30/7/3 buyer pyramid is a teaching model, not measured campaign audience composition. Use customer language, interviews, queries, behavior, or actual results to diagnose awareness.

## Feature → mechanism → benefit → proof

Specify the triggering situation and exact barrier, not merely demographics. Example: receipt scanning → scan once and assign items → settle dinner without a spreadsheet → real capture from receipt to itemized total. The last part is a shot, not decorative B-roll.

For low-budget work, choose a claim you can demonstrate with today's resources. Founder footage or real captures can be enough. Do not require a face, celebrity, generated avatar, or expensive set merely because a reference used one. The deck's creator prices and "first 20 yourself" advice are contextual examples, not universal budgets or prerequisites.
