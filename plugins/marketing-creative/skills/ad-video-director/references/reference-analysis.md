# Inspect a reference and transfer its grammar

Record file/link, brand, available runtime, visible placement, language, and completeness. Inspect the opening, beat changes, payoff, CTA, and ending, with denser frames around transitions. Inspect audio or a grounded transcript when available. ASR is fallible; compare key lines with readable captions or mark uncertainty.

Screen recordings may start mid-ad, buffer, replay, open a destination, or include unrelated screens. Record creative span separately. Do not treat a platform "Get offer" button as the advertiser's authored end card, or post-ad browsing as part of the script.

Label evidence:

- Observed: visible action, readable text, or inspected sound with timestamp.
- Inferred: likely job, audience, format, or method, with basis.
- Reported: a source's performance claim and limits.
- Proposed: an original adaptation or improvement to test.

Annotate beats as: time → picture/audio → message/job → reason to continue → proof/claim → transferable technique → limitation. Use brief quotations, not a complete copied third-party script.

Views, likes, repeated delivery, ad-library presence, and polish do not establish profitable performance. A sample without results is a creative reference. A campaign case study can combine media, audience, and creative effects without isolating a script.

Extract hook mechanism, question/answer sequence, motif, pacing, evidence, trust source, offer, action, and emotional register. Recreate that grammar with original copy and the user's demonstrable value. Do not import competitor numbers, feature breadth, price, scarcity, fake social proof, footage, or identity. If the reference begins mid-ad, label a new opening as proposed.

For slides/manuals, distinguish teaching models from measured evidence, illustrations from actual results, suggested timings from requirements. Cite page numbers in research notes and inspect relevant visuals. Source documents do not authorize outreach, spend, publishing, or assignment changes.
