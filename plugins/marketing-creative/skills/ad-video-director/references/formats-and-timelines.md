# Formats and editable timing maps

Timings below are original planning heuristics, not empirically established optima. Adapt to actual dialogue, task duration, goal, and placement. Preserve the argument in cutdowns; speeding up the long version can destroy comprehension.

| Format | Strong fit | Required evidence | Tradeoff |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Real demo / capture | Consideration, activation, B2B | Input → action → useful output; readable focal area | Tiny UI can hide the benefit |
| Creator / founder demo | Trust, skepticism, nuanced objections | Person using product plus relevant proof | A scripted actor is not a customer testimonial |
| Comment reply / FAQ | Retargeting, specific barrier | Authentic authorized question or labeled editorial question; answer in product | Social styling cannot manufacture credibility |
| Motion-graphics story | Positioning, portfolio, app reveal | One motif, focused product evidence, brand identity | Can become a feature carousel without a throughline |
| POV / skit | Awareness, recognizable frustration | Buyer situation and believable product-linked resolution | Entertainment can overshadow attribution |
| Comparison / challenge | Differentiation | Fair conditions, concrete task, accurate alternatives | Needs stronger factual support |
| Testimonial / case study | Trust, conversion | Permission, real experience, relevant context | Exceptional results are not typical by default |
| Text / no-face | Low-resource tests, simple ideas | Clear hierarchy and a demonstrable outcome | Reading competes with UI and captions |

## Maps by duration and job

**6 seconds — one message / reminder / demo.** 0–1: situation or result with product cue. 1–4: one product action and consequence. 4–6: brand, benefit, next step. One idea; do not squeeze five full persuasion blocks into six seconds. A bumper's memory goal differs from cold acquisition.

**15 seconds — focused problem-to-proof or product-first action.** 0–2: hook. 2–4: context or product setup. 4–9: one complete demo. 9–12: proof or objection. 12–15: action and readable hold. Warm audiences may need offer/proof instead of setup. The deck's 0–3 / 3–6 / 6–11 / 11–14 / 14–15 map is another starting point; one second may be insufficient for a CTA to be read and spoken.

**30 seconds — consideration / activation.** 0–3: buyer tension or useful result. 3–7: specific gap in the old approach. 7–18: one task demonstrated in meaningful stages, with an early result. 18–24: proof, limitation, or key objection. 24–30: action and what happens after it. Prefer one coherent use case to a feature tour.

**30 seconds — motion-graphics story.** 0–3: visual transformation tied to desire. 3–6: one-line positioning. 6–15: two or three linked benefit demos. 15–22: concrete proof/use-case payoff. 22–26: return to central promise. 26–30: distinctive product/brand lockup and action. Visual motifs can change while text and evidence retain enough reading time.

**45 seconds — conversion with an objection.** 0–3: precise tension/FAQ/result. 3–8: context. 8–21: product demo with early value. 21–31: proof and objection. 31–38: real offer/conditions or invitation to inspect the relevant plan. 38–45: recap and action. A soft action can appear earlier.

**60–90 seconds — considered purchase / creator explanation.** 0–3: thesis or objection. 3–10: stakes and concrete preview. 10–25: first demonstrated value. 25–40: deeper mechanism or new evidence and a meaningful re-hook. 40–55: objection and actual terms. 55–70: fit, outcome, action. Extend toward 90 only for more decision-relevant evidence. For a chosen 75-second runtime, allocate the entire 75 deliberately; do not leave the last five seconds unexplained.

## Goal changes ordering

- Awareness: lead with situation, reversal, or distinctive visual; attribute the benefit early and at the payoff. Do not reserve the only brand cue for the end card.
- Consideration: lead with task/result; show how it works. The demo is the story.
- Conversion: establish product/offer context quickly; use the body for proof and friction. A credible low-friction step can outperform a forced hard sell, but must be tested.
- Retargeting: start at the unresolved barrier; use saved setup time for proof and terms.
- Activation: demonstrate the first useful action after install, not unrelated functionality.

## Cutdowns

Start from the shortest complete argument. Preserve hook, attribution, essential proof, action. Six seconds may need a new job and script. Fifteen seconds can answer one objection from a longer ad. A format change is an execution/concept test, not an isolated hook test.
