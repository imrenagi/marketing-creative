# Hooks that lead to the intended belief

Design what the viewer sees, reads, and hears together. Establish one idea, with complementary information rather than competing messages. Begin on action or tension; introductions and logo bumpers must earn their duration.

A useful hook contains a recognizable situation and an expectation worth resolving. Audience, pain, result, and time help formulate an angle, but cramming all four into three seconds hurts comprehension. Time-bound outcomes need specific evidence.

| Mechanism | Opening | Reason to continue | Required payoff | Failure mode |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Result first | Show useful output immediately | How was that done? | Replay the mechanism | Fabricated output or irrelevant spectacle |
| Pain / POV | Show the precise moment of friction | Could there be a better way? | Demonstrate the fix | Generic anxiety with no buyer relevance |
| Skeptic reversal | Question a familiar approach and set up a test | Does this actually differ? | Fair demonstration with limits | "Everything else fails" without evidence |
| Demo challenge | Start a task with a clear constraint | Can it complete the task? | Real input, steps, output | Concealed effort or conditions |
| Objection / FAQ | Put the actual barrier on screen | My concern may be resolved | Evidence specific to that barrier | Fake comment presented as authentic |
| Before/after | Show comparable states | What changed? | Honest provenance and conditions | Exceptional outcome implied to be typical |
| Contrarian lesson | Challenge a relevant assumption | Is my approach wrong? | Explain and demonstrate | Provocation without evidence |
| Micro-lesson | Teach one useful thing | Immediate value | Complete lesson and product bridge | Content unrelated to the product |
| Founder reason | Start with the problem that led to building it | Does this founder understand me? | Product resolving that problem | Biography before buyer relevance |
| Offer / occasion | Real offer or near-term task | Is this the moment for me? | Actual terms and destination | False scarcity or unclear eligibility |

For exploration, usually draft six to ten hooks across at least three mechanisms; fewer for a quick draft. Change the buyer tension or visual premise, not synonyms. Each hook includes:

`first frame | exact words | buyer/goal fit | expectation | payoff time | evidence needed`

Prefer short text readable at phone size. The deck's eight-word guideline is useful for an opening overlay, not a universal limit across languages or placements. A spoken line can cross three seconds when its visual idea is understood immediately.

Select on buyer relevance, comprehension, product connection, credibility, and producibility. Recommend a supported hypothesis, not a known winner. Keep the opening promise consistent with the body.

An open loop needs an early partial payoff and satisfying resolution. Do not withhold all product value until 80–90% merely because a teaching slide suggests that position. Showing a result at second one can create curiosity about the mechanism.

A re-hook adds evidence, a complication, an objection, or a revealing detail. "Wait until the end" adds nothing. A 4–6-second re-hook is a testable option, not a mandatory edit.
