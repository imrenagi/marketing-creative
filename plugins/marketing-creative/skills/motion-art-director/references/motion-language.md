# Motion language

## Camera and continuity

The camera follows an action, reveals a relationship, compares states or isolates proof. Establish a wide view, approach the object carrying new information, and return when context matters. A breathing drift does not replace meaningful shot-size changes. Keep foreground and background under a coherent camera rig; lens overlays can live outside it.

Continuous worlds retain a visual thread. Character stages allow motivated cuts between complete environments. A static camera is valid when objects carry the action. Avoid global camera motion that makes UI unreadable.

## Typography

Choose entrances from meaning: word accumulation, directional reveal, mask, depth resolve, per-word rotation or intentional typing inside an actual input. Use explicit start/end states so seeking backwards works. Preserve shaping for complex scripts; do not blindly split Mandarin, Arabic or combining characters into Latin-style letter animations.

Two clear levels often suffice, but UI labels and evidence documents may need more. Hierarchy is judged by attention, not a universal node count. Keep emphasis selective. Captions and emphasis overlays serve different functions.

## Transitions

Use object relay, match cut, whip following motion, push-through, field wipe, spatial reveal, hard cut or soft dissolve as the story requires. Allocate expensive signature effects sparingly. Avoid a repeated fade/scale pattern that changes only text. Do not reject a useful cut because it lacks a 3D transition.

## Background and surface

Choose a quiet texture, gradient, parallax layer, light sweep, purposeful grid or still field. Motion must not compete with proof. Seed randomness once with a fixed seed; derive positions from timeline time rather than accumulated velocity. No render-time clocks, input dependence or network assets.

## Timing and sound

Movement has a cause, travel and readable settle. Large objects can need longer settling than small details. Avoid forcing all beats to the same speed. Sound can anticipate or confirm an action; do not add a whoosh to every entrance. Source preparation belongs to motion-voice; placed-track mixing uses the framework audio tools.

## Diagnose “looks like slides”

Look for one picture and one sentence replaced on every beat, small subjects in empty space, no state change, repeated ornamental transitions, competing text and camera motion without a reveal. Fix the mechanism: show an action, isolate proof, stage progressive discovery, follow a subject, simplify hierarchy or choose an intentional cut. Do not treat movement quantity as a quality metric.
