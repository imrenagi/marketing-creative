# Explainer approaches

| Approach | Construction | Watch for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Continuous action | One subject moves through a process; state/world changes explain causality | Repetitive drift and unreadable moving labels |
| Cartoon collage | Layered cutouts, paper world, phrase-driven accumulation | One cutout + one sentence becoming a slideshow |
| Visual journalism | Sourced photos/documents, annotations, maps and source tags | Illustrative imagery mistaken for evidence |
| White catalog | Product cutouts on a clear field; detail and comparisons | Decorative clutter obscuring the product |
| Vintage sketch | Consistent engraving/linework, paper and historic type cues | Invented portraits or “historical evidence” |
| Character stage | Complete per-scene environments, articulated characters, local camera | Tiny wide worlds, disconnected joints, motion without action |

Choose imagery for the job: real captures for recognition/evidence, illustration for abstraction, supplied/generated footage for context with truthful labeling. A threat need not always be photoreal. Captions follow placement and accessibility needs, not a style-wide ban.

For character stages, organize joints around stable pivots; animate local transforms from explicit states. Show actions that explain the mechanism. A new sentence can add an object in the same stage rather than triggering a cut. Read framing at every requested ratio; local close-ups can keep characters and mechanisms legible.

For collage, progressively add/rearrange related elements. A common spatial world can connect photographs without implying they document one event. Source labels and ending credits must be readable in each export.
